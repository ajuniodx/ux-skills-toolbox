# UX Strategy

## 1. What strategy is
Strategy is a set of choices about where to play and how to win, under constraints. A list of features or goals is not a strategy.

## 2. The stack
**Mission/Vision** (long-term why) → **Strategy** (the bets) → **Roadmap** (sequence of problems to solve) → **Goals/OKRs** (how we measure progress). Derive goals from the roadmap, not the other way round, to avoid short-term thinking.

## 3. Evaluating a strategy
For each bet ask: How does it **delight** customers? Is it **hard to copy** (brand, network effects, scale, switching costs, unique tech)? Does it improve **margins**? What would have to be true? Which are the 2–3 riskiest assumptions and how will we test them?

## 4. Product-market fit & ICP
- Signals: cohort retention curves that flatten, organic growth, "very disappointed" ≥ 40% if the product disappeared.
- Define the **Ideal Customer Profile** from your best-retained, highest-value customers; design, message and say no based on it.
- Fit is not static: market, channel and model must also fit each other.

## 5. Metrics that matter (SaaS)
MRR broken into new, expansion, contraction, churn; net revenue retention; cohort retention; CAC and payback; LTV:CAC; activation rate. Watch revenue concentration.

## 6. MVP
Smallest thing that tests the riskiest assumption with real users and still delivers value. Define the learning goal and kill/continue criteria before building.

## 7. Avoid the feature trap
Measure outcomes, not output. Each roadmap item states the problem and expected outcome; review results after launch and remove what doesn't work.

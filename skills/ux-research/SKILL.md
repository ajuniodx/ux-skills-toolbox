# UX Research

## 1. Research brief (always)
Background, decision the research will inform, research questions, method, participants (criteria + count), timeline, owner, how results will be shared.

## 2. Choose the method
| Question | Method |
|---|---|
| Why / what needs? | Interviews, contextual inquiry, diary study |
| Can they use it? | Usability test (moderated or unmoderated) |
| How many / how much? | Survey, analytics, A/B test |
| Which option? | Preference/first-click test, A/B |
Qualitative for discovery (5–8 people per segment), quantitative for size and confidence.

## 3. Good questions
- Ask about past behavior, not hypotheticals: "Tell me about the last time you…".
- Open, neutral, one idea per question; no leading ("Don't you think…?").
- Follow up with "Why?", "Can you show me?", "What happened next?". Tolerate silence.
- Never ask users to design the solution or predict whether they'd buy.

## 4. Assumptions first
Plot assumptions on **importance × evidence**. Research the important + low-evidence quadrant first.

## 5. Recruiting
Screener with behavioral criteria, mix of current and non-users, incentive stated upfront, consent and data-handling explained. Over-recruit ~20% for no-shows.

## 6. JTBD interviews
Reconstruct the switch timeline: first thought → passive looking → active looking → decision → first use. Capture the four forces: push of the situation, pull of the new solution, anxiety, habit.

## 7. Synthesis
- Write **nuggets**: one observation + evidence (quote/clip) + tags (persona, journey step, severity).
- **Affinity mapping**: one note per observation, cluster bottom-up, name clusters as insights, then write "How might we…" questions.
- Customer requests are input, not instructions: look for the underlying problem and weight evidence by strength.

## 8. Prioritize
- **2x2** (impact × effort), **MoSCoW** for scope, **dot voting** for fast group convergence, **Now / Next / Later** to communicate a roadmap without dates.
